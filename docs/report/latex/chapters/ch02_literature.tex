\section{Literature Review and Study of Existing Systems}
\label{ch:lit}

\subsection{Study of Existing Systems}
\textbf{Offline messengers.} \emph{Bridgefy}, a Bluetooth mesh messenger used in large protests, is analysed as Study~11 in Table~\ref{tab:studies}; \emph{FireChat} gave phone-to-phone messaging and is discontinued; \emph{Briar} has no server and synchronises offline over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi with end-to-end encryption~\cite{briar}. \textbf{Dedicated radios.} \emph{goTenna Mesh} uses paired radios; \emph{Meshtastic} runs on LoRa with managed flood routing, suppressing a rebroadcast when a neighbour already repeats it~\cite{meshtastic}. \textbf{Research and humanitarian systems.} The \emph{Serval Project} builds disaster phone meshes with \emph{Rhizome} store-and-forward~\cite{serval}; the \emph{DTN architecture} provides store-carry-forward across intermittent links~\cite{fall2003dtn,rfc4838}. ``n/d'' means not documented in the sources consulted.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Systems}
Hop count, the default metric of the surveyed protocols, picks lossy links~\cite{decouto2003etx}; none prioritises an emergency message at every hop; ad hoc routing drops a message with no route while delay-tolerant systems ignore link quality and priority; and deployed messengers publish little measurement, one with no effective confidentiality or authenticity~\cite{albrecht2021bridgefy}.
\subsection{Identified Research or Engineering Gap}
\label{sec:gap}
\textbf{No surveyed system combines priority-aware, link-quality-weighted routing with a delay-tolerant fallback and is evaluated quantitatively under controlled failure.}
\subsection{Proposed Improvements}
EcoMesh routes over the cost $1+5(1-q)+3f$ (Section~\ref{sec:cost}), re-applies SOS priority at every hop, keeps unroutable messages in a bounded queue that never evicts an SOS for routine traffic, authenticates and encrypts with vetted primitives, and measures all of it, including a paired weighted-versus-hop-count experiment.
