\section{Literature Review and Study of Existing Systems}
\label{ch:lit}

\subsection{Study of Existing Systems}
\textbf{Offline messengers.} \emph{Bridgefy} is a Bluetooth mesh messenger used in large protests; its security analysis is Study~14 in Table~\ref{tab:studies}. \emph{FireChat} gave phone-to-phone messaging without a tower and is no longer maintained. \emph{Briar} has no central server: online it relays through Tor, offline it synchronises directly between devices over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi with end-to-end encryption, so delivery happens when two devices connect~\cite{briar}. \textbf{Dedicated radios.} \emph{goTenna Mesh} uses its own paired radios; \emph{Meshtastic} runs on LoRa with \emph{managed flood routing}: every radio rebroadcasts up to a hop limit but suppresses its rebroadcast if a neighbour is already repeating it~\cite{meshtastic}. \textbf{Research and humanitarian systems.} The \emph{Serval Project} builds phone meshes for disaster settings (first with B.A.T.M.A.N., later its own routing) with \emph{Rhizome}, a store-and-forward data layer~\cite{serval}; the \emph{DTN architecture} provides store-carry-forward delivery across intermittent links~\cite{fall2003dtn,rfc4838}. In the tables, ``n/d'' means not documented in the sources consulted.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Systems}
Hop count is the default metric of the surveyed routing protocols, although measurement shows that it picks lossy links~\cite{decouto2003etx}. None prioritises an emergency message over routine traffic at every hop. Ad hoc routing drops a message when no route exists, whereas delay-tolerant systems retain it but ignore link quality and priority. Deployed offline messengers publish little or no measurement, and at least one had no effective confidentiality or authenticity~\cite{albrecht2021bridgefy}.

\subsection{Identified Research or Engineering Gap}
\label{sec:gap}
Existing consumer meshes flood or use simple hop-count routing, offer no message prioritisation and publish little measurement; routing protocols ignore retention, and delay-tolerant systems ignore link quality and priority. \textbf{No surveyed system combines priority-aware, link-quality-weighted routing with a delay-tolerant fallback and is evaluated quantitatively under controlled failure.}

\subsection{Proposed Improvements}
EcoMesh routes over the cost $1+5(1-q)+3f$ (Section~\ref{sec:cost}); re-applies SOS priority at every hop; keeps unroutable messages in a bounded queue that evicts the lowest priority first and never an SOS in favour of routine traffic; authenticates and encrypts relayed messages with vetted primitives; and measures all of it on a seeded simulator and on separate devices, including a paired weighted-versus-hop-count experiment.
