\subsection{Conclusion}
Every mainstream messaging tool fails when its infrastructure fails, although devices in a stricken building can still reach each other. We built EcoMesh, a peer-to-peer mesh in which every device is an endpoint and a relay, with a complete engine (discovery, link-state routing, reliability, security, storage), a simulator that measures it, a REST/WebSocket interface, two front ends, and a multi-device deployment of separate processes exchanging bytes over TCP. Weighted routing delivered \HopW\,\% of messages where hop-count routing delivered \HopH\,\% on identical seeds; an SOS had a \SosAdv$\times$ lower median latency under load; a 40-node mesh at 20\,\% loss with injected failures delivered \CollDel\,\% over 50 runs; and on five live processes an SOS arrived in a median of 47\,ms, the sender re-routed around a failed relay in about 4\,s, and every message held during a gateway outage was delivered when the gateway returned.

\subsection{Achievement of Objectives}
Objectives O1--O3 and O5--O8 were achieved. O4 was achieved for delivery but only partly for recovery time, where 27 of 50 trials exceeded 10\,s by at most 0.25\,s because recovery is bounded by the 10\,s heartbeat timeout; the evidence for each objective is in Table~\ref{tab:objectives}.

\subsection{Major Engineering Contributions}
A transport-agnostic engine that is identical on a simulator, on loopback and on separate laptops; a derived link-quality cost function with an immediate failure penalty and network-wide re-flooding; per-hop priority with a bounded queue that never evicts an SOS for routine traffic; signatures that survive multi-hop relaying; and a measurement framework in which every number in this report is generated from per-node logs and stored results.


\subsection{Current Limitations}
The link is a neighbour-restricted LAN rather than a radio; one pre-shared key serves the mesh; relays drop rather than retain when no route exists; recovery and convergence are quantised by timer constants; SNF-3 and LOG-5 are only partly met; there is no timed usability study or automated UI test; live timings were taken on loopback; and scale was tested to 65 simulated nodes (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).


\subsection{Future Enhancements}
(1) A Bluetooth LE or Wi-Fi Direct transport and an Android client speaking the same protocol. (2) Shorter and adaptive timers: a neighbour timeout near 6--7\,s, an immediate report on topology change and an ACK timeout derived from the measured round-trip time, re-measured with the same scripts. (3) X25519 per-pair key agreement. (4) Short retention at relays and an immediate flush on a new neighbour. (5) Per-destination route invalidation. (6) A usability study, automated browser tests and condition polling in timing-sensitive tests. (7) Tests beyond 65 nodes.

\subsection{Lessons Learned}
Build the simulator first and draw the transport boundary before the layers: it paid for itself in the simulator, the unit tests and the live deployment. Integration finds what unit tests do not (the multi-hop ACK bug survived every single-node test). Look at distributions as well as means: recovery clustered at the heartbeat timeout, which the mean hid. Record who built what from the first week, with consistent Git identities.
